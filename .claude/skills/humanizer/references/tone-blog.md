# Tone: Blog

**Use for:** personal blog posts, essays, technical writeups, newsletter issues, public notes.

**Person and personality level:** `full`. "I" for the author, "you" for the reader. Pick the pair
and hold it for the whole piece.

**Sentence rhythm:** strongly varied, and this is what separates the tone from every other one
here. A one-line paragraph for emphasis is fine. So are parentheticals, asides, and the author
interrupting themselves. The failure mode is a piece that runs at one even mid-length cadence
from top to bottom.

**Terminology:** use the words the audience already uses. Explaining a term they know is padding.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **The SEO opener.** "In this post, we'll explore...", "Have you ever wondered...", "Chances are
  you've run into...". Start with the content.
- **The CTA closer.** "Thanks for reading!", "Let me know in the comments", "If you found this
  useful, share it". §25 covers the generic uplift ending; this is its blog-specific form. Stop on
  the last thing you actually had to say.
- **§33 clusters here** more than anywhere else, because the fake-candid hook reads as blog voice.
- **Opinions sanded off.** A blog with no position reads like documentation with a first person
  glued on. The place where the author says "I still don't like this approach and I can't fully
  justify why" is the part to keep, not the part to smooth.
- **Manufactured pros and cons symmetry,** three bullets a side because three looks complete.

The line to hold: stance is voice and you may add it. Facts are not. "I find this API awkward" is
voice; "it took me three days to get it working" is a fact and belongs only if the source says so.

## Before and after

**Before:** In this post, we'll explore why caching is such a game-changer for modern web
applications. Caching stores the result of an expensive computation so that later requests can
reuse it. Thanks for reading, and let me know your thoughts in the comments!

**After:** Caching stores the result of an expensive computation so later requests can reuse it.
That is the whole idea, and it is also where the trouble starts, because now two copies of the
answer exist and they can disagree.
