# Tone: Peer review

**Use for:** conference and journal reviews, author rebuttals, meta-reviews, written feedback on a
colleague's draft.

**Person and personality level:** `none` for style, but the text must take positions. A review
that sounds evenhanded about everything is useless to the authors and to the chair. In reviews use
"the paper" and "the authors", with "I" or "the reviewer" per venue convention. In rebuttals use
"we" for the authors and the venue's reviewer labels (R1, R2) for the reviewers.

**Sentence rhythm:** twelve to twenty-five words. One claim per bullet or paragraph, each anchored
to a section, equation, table, or line number so the authors can find what you mean.

**Terminology:** use the paper's own terms and notation, even where you would name things
differently. A review that renames the method is a review the authors have to decode.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **Compliment padding as an opener.** "The paper is well written and easy to follow" used as a
  warm-up before the real content. Write it only when it is an actual finding.
- **Criticism with no locus.** "The evaluation is not convincing", "the novelty is limited". Point
  at the missing baseline, the table, the claim on page four. An unanchored objection cannot be
  answered, so it does no work.
- **§24 hedging until the verdict disappears.** "It could perhaps be argued that the contribution
  may be somewhat incremental." Either it is incremental or it is not; say which.
- **§22 sycophancy in rebuttals.** "We thank the reviewer for their insightful and valuable
  comments" as a default opener, and "We completely agree" immediately before disagreeing.
- **Rebuttals that restate the paper** instead of answering what was asked. Answer first, then
  point to where in the paper or to the new result.

## Before and after

**Before:** The paper is well written and easy to follow. However, it could perhaps be argued that
the experimental evaluation may not be entirely convincing, and the novelty appears to be somewhat
limited compared to existing work in this area.

**After:** The evaluation does not convince me that the reported gain comes from the proposed
component.

Then name the locus: which baseline is missing, which table, which claim on which page. That anchor
comes from your own reading of the paper. Never invent one to make the objection sound specific.
