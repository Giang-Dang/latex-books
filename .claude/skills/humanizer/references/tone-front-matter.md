# Tone: Front matter

**Use for:** preface, foreword, introduction-as-front-matter, acknowledgments, "how to read this
book".

**Person and personality level:** `full`. First person, the most personal register in the book:
the author talking about the book, before the book's own voice takes over.

**Sentence rhythm:** conversational and warm, but structured. A preface runs one to three pages,
and every paragraph answers one of the reader's real questions: why this book exists, who it is
for, what it assumes, how to read it.

**Economy:** `standard`. The one-to-three-page bound already does most of the work. Within it a
paragraph may take its time, because a preface is the author talking rather than the book
teaching. See ECONOMY in `SKILL.md`.

**Terminology:** plain. The book's technical vocabulary starts in chapter one; the preface may
name the topic but does not teach it.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **§25 uplift.** "I hope this book inspires you on your journey." End on something concrete:
  what the reader will be able to do, or where to start reading.
- **Overpromising.** "This book will transform the way you think about...". State what the book
  covers and for whom; the chapters do the transforming.
- **The TOC restated.** "Chapter 1 introduces... Chapter 2 covers... Chapter 3 explores..." for
  every chapter is the outline wearing prose. Group the parts and say how to travel them: which
  to read in order, which are reference.
- **Generic gratitude.** "I thank my family, friends, and colleagues for their support." Thanks
  that name no one thank no one. Name people and what each one actually did.
- **The apology opener.** "Many books have been written about X, and I hesitated to add
  another." Say instead what sent you writing this one.

## Before and after

**Before:** I hope this book will transform your journey with distributed systems. Chapter 1
introduces the basics. Chapter 2 covers consensus. Chapter 3 explores replication. I thank
everyone who supported me along the way.

**After:** I wrote this book because the outage that taught me the most had no book behind it;
everything useful was scattered across postmortems. Part I is the theory I wish I had read
first. Parts II and III are reference: start with whichever failure you are living through.
Thanks to Lan, who read every draft twice, and to my old SRE team for letting the real numbers
into print.
