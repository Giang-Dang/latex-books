# Tone: Work

**Use for:** email, Slack messages that matter, status reports to an advisor or manager, internal
proposals, handoff notes.

**Person and personality level:** `restrained`. "I" for yourself, "we" for the team, and address
the recipient directly. Hold one choice for the whole message.

**Sentence rhythm:** twelve to twenty-five words. The first sentence states the thing. Numbers and
dates come before the explanation, because most readers stop after two lines.

**Terminology:** keep the jargon, but write so that someone outside the project can still tell what
the outcome was and what decision is being asked for.

If the source does not make the recipient and the relationship clear, ask once before drafting.
The register for an advisor, a manager, and a peer are three different registers, and guessing
wrong costs more than any style defect in this file.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **Openers that delay.** "Hope this finds you well", "I just wanted to reach out", "Quick question
  for you". One line of greeting is plenty.
- **Burying the ask.** If the reader has to do something, it goes in the first two lines, not under
  three paragraphs of context.
- **Over-apologizing,** or apologizing for something that has not happened yet.
- **§13 passive used to soften bad news.** "The deadline was missed." Say who, how far behind, what
  caused it, and what happens next.
- **Closing with "let me know if you have any questions"** in place of the actual question.

## Before and after

**Before:** Hope this finds you well! I just wanted to circle back on the experiments.
Unfortunately, some delays were encountered due to an issue with the data loader, and the timeline
has slipped by about a week. Please let me know if you have any questions.

**After:** The experiments are about a week behind. A bug in the data loader caused it, and that is
fixed now.

If the source contains a decision you need from the reader, end with it, phrased so it can be
answered in one line. Do not invent a question to fill the slot.
