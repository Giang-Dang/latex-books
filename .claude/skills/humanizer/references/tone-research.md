# Tone: Research

**Use for:** conference and journal papers, theses, related work sections, seminar reports,
abstracts, proposal text.

**Person and personality level:** `none`. Use "we" even for a solo-authored paper if the venue
expects it, or the impersonal ("This chapter presents...") if the thesis forbids first person.
"We" walking the reader through a derivation ("we now bound the error") is standard and not a tell.
Never "I think" or "in my opinion".

**Sentence rhythm:** long is correct here. Twenty to thirty-five words with subordinate clauses is
the register, not a defect. A run of short punchy sentences reads as blog voice leaking in. No
rhetorical questions.

**Terminology:** use the field's exact term and never vary it. §11 is a correctness bug in this
tone, not a style preference: two names for one construct reads as two constructs. Define a term
once, at first use.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **Overclaiming past the evidence.** "significantly", "substantially", "dramatically",
  "demonstrates that" with no test statistic or number behind them. Give the number, or write
  "higher" and leave it there.
- **The stock opening sentence.** "Recent years have seen growing interest in...", "With the rapid
  development of...". This is §1 wearing an academic costume.
- **"To the best of our knowledge"** used to skip a literature search rather than to mark a
  genuine gap.
- **Related work as a name parade.** "Smith et al. [3] proposed X. Jones et al. [4] proposed Y."
  Each entry needs to say how it bears on this work, or it does not belong.
- **§5 and §21 bite hardest here.** Uncited "it is widely believed that", and limitation sections
  that say "further research is needed" instead of naming the limitation. §24 usually clusters in
  the same paragraph.

## Before and after

**Before:** Recent years have seen growing interest in continual learning. Our method significantly
outperforms prior approaches, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

**After:** Our method scores higher than the prior approaches we compare against.

If the source has the numbers, put them here instead of "higher". If it does not, leave the plain
claim and do not borrow "significantly" to fill the gap.
