# Tone: Narrative

**Use for:** popular science and trade nonfiction: story-led chapters for a general reader,
history-of-an-idea sections, profiles, reported scenes.

**Person and personality level:** `full`. First person where the author was in the story, close
third person where they were not. The reader is told a story, not given instructions.

**Sentence rhythm:** the widest range of any book tone. Scene-setting sentences can run long; the
turn of a story can be one line. The failure mode is every paragraph ending on a quotable closer
(§31 lives here).

**Terminology:** translate or cut jargon. One technical term per scene, introduced through the
story that needs it. A general reader holding three new terms to finish a paragraph is reading
exposition, not narrative.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **§1 clusters here worst.** "a testament to human ingenuity", "changed our understanding
  forever", "marked a turning point". Significance is shown by what happened next in the story.
- **§32 aphorism formulas.** "Memory is the architecture of the self." Replace with the concrete
  claim the story actually supports.
- **§31 staccato drama.** "Then everything changed. No warning. Nothing." One short sentence
  turns a story; a run of them is a trailer voice-over.
- **§12 false ranges.** "from the smallest microbe to the vastness of space".
- **The omniscient feelings report.** "Little did she know...", "He must have felt...". Report
  what the record supports: what people did, said, and wrote. Invented interiority is invented
  fact.
- **Scene-free abstraction.** Pages of ideas with no person, place, or time attached. If nothing
  happens anywhere to anyone, the chapter is an essay wearing a narrative jacket.

## Before and after

**Before:** Little did the team know that this discovery would change everything. It was not just
a result. It was a revolution. Their work stands as a testament to the power of human curiosity.

**After:** The team ran the detector for three nights before anyone developed the plates. The
third plate contradicted the textbook all of them had learned from, and the argument it started
ran for a decade.
