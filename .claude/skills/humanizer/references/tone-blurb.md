# Tone: Blurb

**Use for:** back cover copy, catalog and store descriptions, landing page copy, the
one-paragraph pitch in a proposal.

**Person and personality level:** `full`, in a register no other tone allows: sales voice. "You"
for the reader being won over, or no person at all. This is marketing copy by design, the only
tone in this skill that is.

**The scoped exception:** §4 (promotional language) and §25 (upbeat endings) are relaxed here,
and only here. A blurb is allowed to sell: to promise a benefit and to end forward. The moment
the text sits inside the book, §4 and §25 apply again in full. §14 stays closed: no em or en
dashes, as everywhere (only a user-provided writing sample reopens it).

**Sentence rhythm:** short and front-loaded. The first sentence decides whether the second gets
read. Around 100 to 200 words for a back cover.

## Tells specific to this tone (still banned while selling)

- **Invented praise** (§2 and the no-invented-facts rule). No endorsements, review quotes, sales
  figures, or "acclaimed" unless the acclaim is real and citable.
- **Empty superlatives.** "The definitive, essential, groundbreaking guide" with no concrete
  hook. Sell with specifics the book can back: what the reader will build, fix, or understand by
  the last chapter.
- **§10 stacking.** "You will learn X, master Y, and unlock Z", three by three by three.
- **§12 false ranges.** "From beginner to expert, from theory to practice".
- **Promises the book does not keep.** Every claim on the cover must be checkable against the
  table of contents. Overselling is a returns policy, not a tone.
- **Explaining the whole plot.** A blurb opens a gap; the book closes it.

## Before and after

**Before:** This groundbreaking, essential guide is a testament to modern engineering. From
beginners to experts, everyone will learn to write queries, master indexing, and unlock
performance. It is not just a book, it is a journey.

**After:** Your queries are slow and the dashboards will not say why. This book teaches you to
read a query plan the way the optimizer does, using the actual plans from twelve production
incidents. By the last chapter, you will diagnose in minutes what used to take your team a week.
