# Tone: Talk

**Use for:** talk scripts, speaker notes under slides, thesis defense, demo narration. This is
text to be said out loud, not read with the eyes.

**Person and personality level:** `restrained`. Match the venue: "I" for a solo talk, "we" for
work done by a team, and address the committee directly in a defense. Pick one and hold it.

**Sentence rhythm:** every sentence sayable in one breath, under roughly twenty words. Break where
a speaker breathes. Repeat the subject instead of using a pronoun that points back two sentences,
because the audience cannot scroll up.

**Terminology:** keep the technical term, but write it the way you will pronounce it. A phrase
that is awkward to say out loud is wrong here even when it reads fine on the page.

## Tells specific to this tone

- **Written sentences that cannot be spoken.** Stacked subordinate clauses, a subject separated
  from its verb by fifteen words. This is the worst failure of the tone and the one to fix first.
- **"As you can see on this slide"** at every transition.
- **Reading the slide verbatim.** The spoken line should carry what the slide cannot hold: why the
  number matters, what went wrong before it, what you want the audience to take away.
- **§28 at every seam.** "Now let's move on to...", "Next, I'd like to talk about...". Transition
  with content instead: state the first fact of the new section and the audience follows.
- **A hollow summary before "thank you".** One closing sentence, and it should be a real
  conclusion.
