\chapter{Hot Chocolate from Zero}
\label{ch:hot-chocolate-zero}

Hot Chocolate derives the schema from your C\# types, so the schema is
something you read back rather than something you write and keep in step.

That arrangement has a cost attached. Every public member is a schema field
until you say otherwise, so the reader of your API sees the consequences of
decisions you made for the compiler.

\index{prerequisites}%
From here on the book assumes three things of you. C\# and ASP.NET Core at the
level of having shipped a controller: every file is printed in full, and none
of the pages explain what a record, a builder or a middleware is. HTTP and
JSON, because every request is printed as raw HTTP and every response as the
JSON that came back. And GraphQL at the level of having sent one query and
read one schema. Nothing assumes you have designed a schema, run a GraphQL
service in production, or met federation before
chapter~\ref{ch:do-you-need-this}. A term a reader at that level may not have
met gets one clause the first time it appears, and the index carries every
one of them.
