\section{Permission to Stop Here}
\label{sec:ch01-permission-to-stop-here}

Before you decide how much weight to put on the case I have just made, here is
what I could not find. No account exists, that I can locate, of a team that
federated its graph,
regretted it, and took it apart: no postmortem, no conference talk, no
engineering blog, from any company. I am not going to tell you what that means,
because I do not know. It is consistent with federation working when it is
adopted for the reasons in section~\ref{sec:ch01-the-threshold}. It is equally
consistent with nobody wanting to publish that particular retrospective. What I
can say is that the public case for federation is made almost entirely by
people who adopted it and by the vendors selling the tooling, and that is worth
holding in mind while you read them. It is worth holding in mind while you read
me.

The honest recommendation does not sell a book about federation.

If the four conditions do not hold, read chapter~\ref{ch:hot-chocolate-zero}
through chapter~\ref{ch:schema-design} and stop. Those three build one Hot
Chocolate service, kill the N+1 problem inside it, and work through the schema
design decisions that hurt later no matter how many services you finish with.
Not one of them assumes a federated graph, and all of them apply to the single
service you should be building instead. Chapter~\ref{ch:federation-model} is
where this book starts splitting things apart, and that is where you should put
it down.

Come back when a second team needs to change your schema and cannot.
