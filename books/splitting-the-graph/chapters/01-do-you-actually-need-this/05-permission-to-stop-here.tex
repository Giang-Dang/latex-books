\section{Permission to Stop Here}
\label{sec:ch01-permission-to-stop-here}

The accounts cited here describe successful adopters. They show what those
teams gained, not how often adoption fails or what your team will gain. Test
your release constraints against the four conditions before treating their
outcomes as a recommendation.

If the four conditions do not hold, read chapter~\ref{ch:hot-chocolate-zero}
through chapter~\ref{ch:schema-design} and stop. Those three build one Hot
Chocolate service, kill the N+1 problem inside it, and work through the schema
design decisions that hurt later no matter how many services you finish with.
Not one of them assumes a federated graph, and all of them apply to the single
service you should be building instead. Chapter~\ref{ch:federation-model} is
where this book starts splitting things apart, and that is where you should put
it down.

Come back when a second team needs to change your schema and cannot.
