\begin{chaptersummary}
  \item Federation solves a coordination problem between teams, not a
        technical problem with GraphQL. Netflix and Booking.com both moved to
        it after one team became the bottleneck for everybody else's schema
        changes.
  \item The trade is a merge performed by people for a merge performed by a
        program. Services publish their own schemas, a composer merges them
        into a supergraph, and a router answers queries across them.
  \item That swap is what everything costs. Four obligations in every
        subgraph, a composition step that fails on other teams' changes, a
        router tier on the request path, and a development loop that now spans
        services.
  \item The coordination does not go away. It moves into ownership of types
        and keys, which is negotiated between the same teams as before and is
        now enforced by the composer.
  \item The threshold is four conditions, not a team count. Nobody has
        published a number, so this one is mine, and the condition worth
        testing hardest is whether your teams can already release without each
        other.
\end{chaptersummary}
