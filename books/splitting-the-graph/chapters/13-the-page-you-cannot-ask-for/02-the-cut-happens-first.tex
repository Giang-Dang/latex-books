\section{The Cut Happens First}
\label{sec:ch13-cut-happens-first}

\index{query plan!page cut before hydration|(}%
Ask for the plan, the way chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router} taught, and it
explains the answer with no special case for paging anywhere in it. The first
step is a \code{Single} fetch to Sessions for one row. The second is a
\code{BatchEntity} fetch to Ratings, carrying a representation of that row,
for its score. By the time Ratings is asked anything, which session is on the
page has been decided by a service that never saw a score.

\index{hydration and discovery}%
Two operations are hiding in what looks like one flat response, and
figure~\ref{fig:ch13-two-page-orders} pulls them apart. Choosing which objects
are on the page is discovery. Filling in fields on objects already chosen is
hydration. Everything the router has done since
chapter~\ref{ch:second-third-subgraph} is the second, and it does the second
well; it does not invent the first, and nothing in a plan could.

\index{query plan!no step replaces a row}%
Read the measured plans for this graph with that split in mind. A root
\code{Single} fetch produces a list. A later \code{BatchEntity} fetch fills
fields on members of that list, at the members' own position in the response.
No step discards a member, reorders the rest, mints new cursors and goes
back to the owner for a replacement row, and the reason is what a replacement
would cost. Suppose Sessions hands back ten rows and six of them score too
low. Returning four rows breaks \code{first: 10}. Fetching ten more does not
settle it, because the next ten may score too low as well, and a router that
kept going would need a scan protocol, a stable sort, ownership of the cursors
and a rule for when to stop, spanning two services and agreed by both. None of
that follows from a client selecting a field.

\index{list owner!the rule}%
So the rule I use for a connection with this plan shape is strict. The service
that owns the list owns its membership, its filtering, its ordering, its
cursors and the slice, and I call it the list owner from here on. A field
another service contributes may decorate the members. It takes no part in
choosing them.
