\chapter{Strangling the Monolith}
\label{ch:strangling-the-monolith}

Chapter~\ref{ch:the-first-cut-extracting-catalog} took one domain out of Mosaic
and chapters~\ref{ch:composition} to~\ref{ch:entity-resolution-done-right} spent
three chapters on what that one cut produced. This chapter takes the other five
out, and then deletes what is left.

That last part is the difference between a refactor and a strangler. The pattern
is named after a fig that grows around its host and stands up on its own when
the host rots away, and the host is supposed to rot. At tag \code{ch12} there is
no \code{src/Mosaic.Api}. There are six services, one database each, and a
router in front of them, and every number chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router} and
chapter~\ref{ch:entity-resolution-done-right} printed about a single process is
now a number about four of them.

Most of the work was mechanical, which is the return on a decision made in
chapter~\ref{ch:hotchocolate-quickly} and held since: no entity in Mosaic holds
a navigation property to a type another domain owns. Six databases out of one
cost no \code{Include} and no dropped constraint. What was not mechanical is
everything this chapter is actually about: which service goes first and why, how
you move a field that is already answering traffic, what six teams share and
what they must never share, and which of the promises the schema makes stop
being checkable the moment the check is on the other side of a network.
