\chapter{Entity Resolution Done Right}
\label{ch:entity-resolution-done-right}

Chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router} watched a router assemble the storefront out
of two services and stopped at the boundary. This chapter goes through it.

Everything Mosaic hands the router comes back through one field.
\code{Query.\_entities} takes a list of representations and answers a list of
objects, and every federated query in this book since
chapter~\ref{ch:how-a-federated-query-actually-runs} has gone through it. What
happens inside that field is the subject here: how many times your resolver is
called, what it is handed, what it is allowed to answer, and what the client
sees when one of the answers is nothing.

Tag \code{ch11} of the companion repository changes Mosaic for the first time
since chapter~\ref{ch:the-first-cut-extracting-catalog}. \code{Product} gains a
field, \code{shippingCost}, and it is the first field in the service that
cannot be answered out of the service: the carrier's rate follows from what the
thing is, and what the thing is belongs to Catalog. That one field is what
\code{@requires} is for, and putting it in produced the non-uniform
\code{dependencies} array chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router} predicted and could
not show.

The tag also adds \code{samples/entity-resolution}, two small subgraphs whose
only job is to be watched. One of them writes down every call its reference
resolver receives, which is how the first section stops being an argument about
DataLoaders and becomes a listing.
