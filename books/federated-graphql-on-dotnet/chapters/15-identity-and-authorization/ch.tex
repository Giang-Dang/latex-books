\chapter{Identity and Authorization Across the Graph}
\label{ch:identity-and-authorization}

Thirteen chapters of building a graph, and anybody who can reach it can read
anything in it. \code{Query.ordersByCustomer} takes a customer identifier and
hands back that customer's orders, to whoever asked. \code{Customer.email} sits
on the storefront, two hops from any product page. \code{submitReview} takes the
author's identifier as an argument and believes it, so a client can post a
review in anybody's name.

None of that is an oversight I am about to apologise for. It is what a graph
looks like when nobody has asked the question yet, and asking it late is the
normal case: authorization is the thing that gets added to a system that already
works, by someone who did not design it, under a deadline. What makes it hard
here is not that Mosaic is unusual. It is that a federated graph has more than
one place to put a rule, and the places do not agree about what a rule can say.

There are three of them. The router can refuse a field from the token alone,
before it fetches anything, using directives the composer copied out of a
subgraph's published schema. The subgraph can refuse the same field in its own
process, using an attribute the composer throws away. And a resolver holding the
row can refuse this particular order to this particular caller, which is the
only rule that matters most of the time and the only one no schema can express.
Getting a field right can mean writing it three times, and nothing in the build
checks that the three agree.

By the end of this chapter Mosaic has all three, a token, and four guarded
fields. It also has two findings I did not expect and would rather have been
wrong about: a directive the specification defines that this composer discards
without a word, and a router setting that turns on authentication for
subscriptions by taking the public storefront offline.

Everything the composer says was said by wgc 0.129.7, which bundles version
0.63.2 of \code{@wundergraph/composition}. The router is Cosmo 0.337.1. Every
line of library source is HotChocolate at tag \code{16.6.0}, commit
\code{8fea46e}. The tokens are HS256, signed with a key that is in a public
repository, which is a development convenience and would be a finding anywhere
else.
