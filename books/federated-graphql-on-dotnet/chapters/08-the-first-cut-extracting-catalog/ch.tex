\chapter{The First Cut: Extracting Catalog}
\label{ch:the-first-cut-extracting-catalog}

Chapter~\ref{ch:how-a-federated-query-actually-runs} ran a federated graph made
of two toy services with three products in them. This chapter takes Mosaic
apart.

Catalog goes first, because Catalog asks nobody for anything. It owns
\code{Product} and it owns nothing else, and the five domains left behind all
speak to it the same way: through an identifier. That was arranged in
chapter~\ref{ch:hotchocolate-quickly}, where every field went into the folder of
the domain that owns it and no entity was given a navigation property to an
entity belonging to another domain. The bill for that arrangement comes due
here, and the interesting thing about the extraction is how much of it was
already paid.

What follows is the real cut, at tag \code{ch08} of the companion repository:
one service becomes two, one database becomes two, and the twenty-five products
this book has been printing since chapter~\ref{ch:hotchocolate-quickly} end up
in a different process from the reviews written about them. No router appears
anywhere in this chapter. Everything is tested one subgraph at a time, by hand,
because a subgraph you cannot exercise on its own is a subgraph you will end up
debugging through a router, which is chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router}'s job and
not a debugger.

Three of the things I had to fix took longer than the extraction did, and none
of the three is visible from inside either service.
