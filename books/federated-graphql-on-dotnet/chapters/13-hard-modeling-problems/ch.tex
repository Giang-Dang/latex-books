\chapter{Hard Modeling Problems}
\label{ch:hard-modeling-problems}

Chapter~\ref{ch:strangling-the-monolith} finished the extraction and left a
graph that works. This chapter is about the things in it that were already
wrong, or already impossible, and that nobody had looked at because the
extraction was the work.

Four of them were sitting in the graph already, and they have nothing obvious in
common. An enum is declared in two subgraphs. A \code{Money} type is declared in
two others. Four types implement \code{Node} and no field in the entire graph
returns one, so the interface is decoration. And \code{Product.reviews} is a
page of one service's rows hanging off another service's entity, which turns out
to work, while the thing you would expect to work next does not. A fifth problem
is one Mosaic does not have and most graphs eventually do: a field that belongs
on every implementation of an interface, contributed by a service that cannot
name any of them.

What they have in common is where the difficulty lives. None of these is a bug
in HotChocolate or in the router, and none of them is fixed by a directive. Each
one is a modelling decision that was free inside one process and costs something
across seven, and the cost is only visible if you go and look for it. Two of
them compose without a word from the composer, which is the part worth being
nervous about.

I also found a defect while measuring the first one, and it had been in the
repository since chapter~\ref{ch:data-without-the-n-plus-1}. It is not a
federation defect. What federation did was hide it.

Everything the composer says in this chapter was said by wgc 0.129.7, which
bundles \code{@wundergraph/composition} 0.63.2, and every line of library source
it reads is HotChocolate at tag \code{16.6.0}, commit \code{8fea46e}. The router
is Cosmo 0.337.1, unchanged since chapter~\ref{ch:enter-the-router}.
